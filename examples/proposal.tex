\section{Summary}
Retrieval practice improves long-term retention in laboratory studies, but its effect in large
introductory courses, where it competes for scarce class time, is less clear. We propose a
two-semester randomised study in a first-year statistics course comparing a two-minute
pre-lecture quiz with an equivalent reading prompt, measured by a delayed retention test.

\section{Aims}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Aim 1.} Estimate the effect of pre-lecture quizzes on retention five weeks after each topic.
  \item \textbf{Aim 2.} Test whether the effect differs by prior attainment.
  \item \textbf{Aim 3.} Measure the class time and staff effort the intervention costs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Design and methods}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\sffamily\bfseries}p{.2\linewidth}X@{}}
Design & Randomisation by tutorial group within each semester; topics alternate between conditions. \\\addlinespace
Participants & Students enrolled in the course who consent; expected size \tbc{enrolment figures}. \\\addlinespace
Outcome & Score on a delayed retention test five weeks after each topic. \\\addlinespace
Analysis & Mixed-effects model with random intercepts for student and topic; effect reported with its 95\% confidence interval. \\\addlinespace
Ethics & Approval from the institutional review board before data collection. \\
